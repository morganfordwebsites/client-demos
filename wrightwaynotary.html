What he signs

Everything a notary handles.

Most appointments are a signature and a stamp. Some are a sixty-page closing. Both get the same preparation, the same straight answers, and the same person on the other end of the phone.

  • 01

    General notarization

    Acknowledgments, jurats, oaths and affirmations, signature witnessing, including real estate and legal documents that arrive with short notice.

  • 02

    Loan signings

    Refinances, purchase and seller packages, HELOCs and reverse mortgages. He prints the package, walks the signer through it, and returns it the way the lender asked.

  • 03

    Apostille & authentication

    Illinois documents prepared for an apostille or certificate of authority when paperwork is headed to another country.

  • 04

    Notarial copies

    Certified copies of original documents, stamped at the table while you wait.

  • 05

    Mobile appointments

    He comes to your home, office, hospital room or care facility. No office visit, no driving across town to find a notary who is open.

  • 06

    Evenings, weekends, after hours

    The line is open at 9 p.m. on a Saturday as readily as 10 a.m. on a Tuesday. Call and ask for the time that works.

How it goes

Four things true of every appointment.

Not a promise invented for a website. These are the habits a client wrote about after he showed up early with the paperwork already checked.

01

On time, or you hear from him first.

He calls the signer if traffic turns. Across five years of reviews, no one has written about standing around wondering where he was.

02

Never rushed, never advised.

He will not hurry a signer through a page or tell anyone what a document means legally. He explains what he is witnessing, not what you should do.

03

The paperwork leaves complete.

Copies for the signer, scans back to the requester, and a drop schedule that respects a next-day deadline. Nothing rides around in a car overnight.

04

The same person every time.

You call Michael, Michael shows up. No dispatch queue, no rotating subcontractor, no explaining the situation twice.

About Michael

Two careers. One very careful stamp.

Michael Wright spent his working life on the clock for other people: a retired Chief Petty Officer in the U.S. Navy, then a career as a Federal Law Enforcement Officer. Wright-Way Notary Service is what he built afterward, out of a home base on Prairie Crossing Drive in Montgomery.

The training carried over. He shows up when he says he will, he reads a document before he signs off on it, and he does not treat a closing like a formality. Title companies keep him on their list for the packages that cannot be redone, including refinances, seller packages, HELOCs and reverse mortgages. Families call him for the harder appointments, like a hospital bedside or a kitchen table the week a parent passes.

Call or text any hour. If he cannot be there himself at the moment you need, he will tell you straight rather than let a deadline slide.
