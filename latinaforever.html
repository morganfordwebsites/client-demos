02 — Translation

Two languages,
one appointment.

Her card says it plainly: Servicios de traducción y notaría en inglés y español. Bring the Spanish document to the English office that wants it — or the other way around.

08 — Questions

What people ask before they book.

If yours isn't here, text it over. Answering costs nothing and saves you a wasted drive.

Do I need an appointment?

Yes — call or text first. The listing says open 24 hours, but the service is mobile, so the time gets confirmed with you.

What ID do I bring?

Current government photo ID — license, passport or state ID — for every signer, plus anyone signing as a witness.

Can you notarize a document in Spanish?

Usually, yes. I'm bilingual, and I check whether the office receiving it needs the certificate worded in English.

Can you certify a copy?

No. Georgia notaries can't certify copies. What I can do is notarize your signature on an affidavit stating the copy is true, if the receiving office accepts that.

Can I sign before you arrive?

No. The signature has to happen in front of me, or the certificate is worthless and you'll pay twice.

Can you tell me which form to use?

I can explain what I'm witnessing, but not what you should sign or how to word it — that's legal advice, and only an attorney can give it.

How much does it cost?

Georgia caps the notarial fee itself at $2 per act (O.C.G.A. § 45-17-11). Mobile travel and translation are quoted separately, up front, before I drive out.

Do you go to hospitals and nursing homes?

Yes, as long as the signer is awake, aware and has ID. Call ahead so the facility and I can coordinate.
