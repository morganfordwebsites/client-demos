FAQs

Questions, Answered Clearly

What ID do I need to bring?
Each signer needs a valid, government-issued photo ID — a driver's license, state ID card, passport, or military ID. The ID should be current (or expired less than [X] years, depending on state rules) and match the name on your document.
Can a notary give me legal advice?
No. Notaries verify identity and witness signatures — we cannot advise on what a document means, whether you should sign it, or how to fill it out. For legal guidance, please consult a licensed attorney. We're happy to explain the notarization process itself, clearly.
How does remote online notarization work?
You'll receive a secure link for a video session. You'll verify your identity with your photo ID (plus a short knowledge-based authentication step for some documents), sign electronically, and we apply a digital seal. The whole process typically takes 10–15 minutes and is legally valid in every state that recognizes RON.

How do I pay?
We accept cash, Zelle, Venmo, and all major credit and debit cards. Payment is due at the time of service, and you'll always know the full price before your appointment begins.
What's your cancellation policy?
Life happens — cancel or reschedule at least [4] hours before your appointment at no charge. Cancellations with less notice may incur a small [$15] trip fee to cover travel already underway.
Do I have to sign in front of the notary?
Yes, for most document types. Signatures generally must be made in the notary's presence (in person or on camera for remote sessions), because we're verifying that you — the identified signer — are the one signing. Please don't pre-sign documents; we'll walk you through exactly when to sign.
My document is incomplete — can it still be notarized?
It depends on what's missing. Blank required fields should be completed before we stamp. If something is unclear, we'll flag it and help you understand what may need attention — though we can't tell you what to write. When in doubt, bring it along and we'll take a look together.
Is my notarization valid in other states?
Generally, yes. A properly executed notarization is valid across state lines, and remote online notarizations are recognized in most states. For documents used internationally, an apostille may also be required — we can guide you through that process.
How long does an appointment take?
Most single-document appointments take 15–20 minutes. Loan signing packages typically run 60–90 minutes. We never rush you — every question you have is worth answering.
What if a signer doesn't have valid ID?
There are alternatives: credible witnesses who can swear to the signer's identity (rules vary by state), or certain affidavit-based procedures. Call us at [PHONE] before the appointment and we'll find the cleanest legal path together.
