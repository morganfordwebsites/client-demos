Questions we get

The short answers.

Can a notarization really happen at 2am?

The listing shows open 24 hours, and the reviews back it up: one customer called at seven in the morning and was signing by 7:30. Call first so the notary can confirm he's free at that hour; after-hours and weekend calls can carry a premium.

Is online notarization legal in Texas?

Yes. Texas allows remote online notarization for most documents, performed over a live audio-video session with identity verification. A few documents still require a physical appearance, and the notary will tell you which category yours falls into before you pay anything.

What do I need to bring?

A current government-issued photo ID (driver's license, state ID, passport or military ID) and the complete document with every page attached. Don't sign it beforehand.

What can a notary not do?

A Texas notary can't give legal advice, choose a document for you, or explain what a document means. That's an attorney's job. The notary verifies identity, witnesses the signature, and completes the certificate.

How much does it cost?

Texas caps the fee at $10 for the first signature on an acknowledgment and $1 for each additional signature, with online notarization capped at $25 plus the notary fee. Mobile visits can add a travel fee, which is quoted on the call. Reviewers repeatedly describe the pricing as fair or better than the alternatives nearby.
