The difference

A walkthrough won't find the mold behind the rim joist.

Mold is a moisture problem first. So we measure the moisture, look inside the wall, and only then test what's actually growing. Whole-home inspections run three to seven hours with one or two inspectors, because the part that affects your health is the part nobody opened.

01

Thermal imaging

We scan ceilings, rim joists, and wall cavities with a thermal camera for temperature differences that mean moisture, not just visible growth.

02

Moisture readings

Pin and pinless meters at every suspect area, so we know whether a wall is wet right now or was wet last spring and never dried out.

03

Mold and mycotoxin testing

Samples are analyzed by an AIHA-accredited lab. Mycotoxin testing goes past a spore count when someone in the house is already sick.

04

A written plan, in order

Findings, photos, and prioritized next steps: what to fix, what to clean, what to leave alone, and what to verify before containment comes down.

Remediation consulting

We don't do remediation. We make sure yours is done right.

Valley Mold Services doesn't swing hammers or set containment. We write the protocol, coach you through the options, and stay on your side of the table while the work happens.

01

Custom remediation protocol

A written protocol built for your house and your situation, to industry and NORMI standards. Hand it to your remediation company, or use it to guide your own work.

02

Post-remediation verification

After the work is finished but before containment comes down, we come back, inspect the remediated areas, and test to confirm the job actually worked.

03

Advocate during remediation

We help you choose a remediation company, answer your questions as they come up, and hold the crew to the standards and the protocol in writing.

04

DIY remediation coaching

Some situations are appropriate to handle yourself. We'll tell you honestly whether yours is one of them, and if it is, give you detailed, customized steps.

Certification

Wisconsin and Minnesota don't require mold licensing. Ours is voluntary: our primary inspector is certified by NORMI (National Organization of Remediators and Mold Inspectors) as both a mold inspector and a mold remediator, one of the three certifying bodies recognized by every state that does require it. We chose NORMI because it acknowledges the health effects of mold, which is the whole reason this business exists.

Our story

This business started in 2017, a few months after we moved into a new-construction house and learned the floor joists had been recalled. The coating on them was off-gassing formaldehyde at extreme levels.

The remediation that followed was botched in a dozen ways. Containment and negative air pressure weren't maintained, the house dropped below freezing mid-job, and the plumbing burst while the ducts were disconnected. Formaldehyde dust went up through the ductwork and settled two storeys up in the bedrooms.

A few years later, house hunting again, we put an offer on a home with a moldy attic: black sheathing, mushrooms growing, insulation gone crunchy. We walked away. The next house passed its inspection with nothing notable reported, and turned out to have mold at the rim joists, a toilet, a sink, several showers, a jetted tub, the mechanical room, and the windows.

Mold has cost our family a lot of money and a lot of health. It didn't have to.

What we learned is that the standards exist, and that somebody has to insist they're followed. Nobody cares about the health of your home more than you do. We've been through five inspections, several remediations, and the recovery after. We do this work so you don't have to learn it the way we did.
